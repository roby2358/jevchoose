# Story

## Voice
Write in the style of a half-drunk 70s sword-and-sandals fantasy writer, torn between pounding out enough words to pay the rent and capturing his grand vision. The voice is gripping, compelling, and a little hypnotic, pulling the reader into the story with no escape.

Concretely: close third person, past tense, one hero. Short muscular sentences broken by the occasional long rolling one. Sweat, bronze, torchlight, the smell of the street. No modern idiom, no irony, no winking at the reader. Every section is action in a scene, not summary; show what the hero sees and does, and let the reader feel the danger before the hero names it.

## Prose
Write the next section as 3 paragraphs, about 300 words in total, separated by blank lines.

- If the reader chose an option, open by playing out that choice in the scene. Do not recap, do not restate the choice; show it.
- Keep continuity with everything already written: names, places, wounds, who has what.
- Each paragraph should move the situation; by the third, something new has been discovered or something dangerous has arrived.
- The last paragraph ends at the exact moment the hero must decide. Stop there. Do not hint at what the options are.

Output only the prose. No headings, no section numbers, no commentary, no list of choices.

## Choices
Output format, exactly:
1. <first option>
2. <second option>
3. <third option>

Nothing else. Each option is one or two sentences, second person imperative, naming a concrete action the hero can take right now in the scene.

The three options must be different kinds of move (for example fight, deceive, flee into worse danger, bargain, break something), not three flavors of the same move. Each must be plausible given the story so far and each must drive the story forward in a meaningful way: a step the hero cannot take back. No retreating, waiting, hiding until it blows over, or returning to safety; every option commits the hero to something new. Do not say what will happen if the option is taken.

## Choose (for jev)

Read `story_so_far`: a serialized sword-and-sandals adventure that has reached a decision point. Read the three `choices`, each a possible next move for the hero. Judge each as a continuation of the story, not on its own, and pick one according to `stage`:

- stage "build" (sections 1 through 8): pick the choice that raises the stakes most while following naturally from what is already written, and that leaves the most story still to tell. Prefer danger and discovery over safety. A choice that contradicts established facts is worse than a dull one. A choice that would resolve the central conflict too early is worse than that. A choice that merely delays is worst of all.
- stage "wrap" (section 9): pick the choice that best resolves the crescendo just reached, pays off what was set up earlier, and lets the hero and reader catch their breath.
- stage "seed" (section 10): pick the choice that most clearly shapes the start of the next story in the series (a new threat, a new goal, or an unfinished debt) while still closing this one.

When two choices are close, prefer the one that puts the hero in the company of another character over one that leaves the hero alone.

## Open

[Thisle is a clever, wiry thief, sharp of wit with a quick tongue and quicker grasp. He is adept with a sword but will seek the shadows if he is able. On this day he has stolen the prize of a lifetime. Now he must wait until nightfall so he can slip over the city walls with his prize. But a strange angry hunter is coming for him to reclaim it. In the dark city streets, chased by a supernatural monstrosity, Thisle finds he has fewer friends in the city than he thought.]

Thisle eased through the market square, sliding nimbly through the bustling crowd as if unbothered by the dense, colliding bodies. His bronze sword bumped against his thigh as he moved along.

A fruit merchant turned to haggle with a customer, and when he did, Thisle's hand darted out, seized a ripe fruit, and tucked it back into his sleeve. His face barely flickered but he felt a happy glow inside.

He reflected that he shouldn't be taking chances with petty theft when in the pouch at his belt he held the dazzling jewel of Barathrane. Having secured it, his top priority was getting out of the city alive, not pilfering fruit.

He raised the fruit to his mouth and took a bite. Then froze. There she was, in the shadowed side of the alley ahead, all sleek curves and dark hair, her eyes scanning the crowd.

He whispered, "Mirza."
